About

I am a Ph.D. student in the Department of Electronic Engineering at Tsinghua University, advised by Prof. Xueqing Li. My work sits between machine learning and computer architecture: I am drawn to problems where the structure of a model and the machine that runs it have to be designed together.

Publications

  1. EGO: Efficient Compression of Unstructured Sparse DNNs for Compute-in-Memory based on Graph Minimum-Cost Matching Optimization

    Teng Wan, Y. Cao, H. Yang, X. Li

    Design, Automation & Test in Europe (DATE), 2026

    Abstract

    Compute-in-memory arrays have a fixed structure, which unstructured sparsity does not match. We cast the compression as a minimum-cost matching problem on a graph, mapping weights onto arrays at almost no accuracy loss and cutting both storage and compute overhead.

  2. ADDR: Architecture Design and Model Deployment Optimization for Hybrid SRAM-ROM Compute-in-Memory

    Teng Wan, Y. Chen, Z. Chen, Y. Liu, H. Yang, X. Li

    Great Lakes Symposium on VLSI (GLSVLSI), pp. 22–28, 2025

    Abstract

    SRAM is flexible but large; ROM is dense but static. Hybridising them lets weights live where their access patterns suit them. Since architecture and deployment are tightly coupled, we search both jointly with reinforcement learning in a high-level design space.

  3. DIAS: Distance-based Attention Sparsity for Ultra-Long-Sequence Transformer with Tree-like Processing-in-Memory Architecture

    Z. Chen, Y. Chen, Teng Wan, T. Yu, Y. Wang, H. Yang, X. Li

    ACM/IEEE Design Automation Conference (DAC), pp. 1–7, 2025

    Abstract

    Distance-aware attention sparsification combined with a tree-like in-memory processing architecture, to accelerate inference for ultra-long sequences.

  4. ROM-SRAM Hybrid Compute-in-Memory for Edge AI: Circuits, Architectures and Challenges

    X. Du, T. Yu, H. Zhou, L. A. Cheong, Teng Wan, H. Yang, X. Li

    The Journal of Supercomputing, 81(17):1606, 2025

    Abstract

    A review of ROM-SRAM hybrid compute-in-memory for edge AI — circuits, architectures and the open challenges between them.
